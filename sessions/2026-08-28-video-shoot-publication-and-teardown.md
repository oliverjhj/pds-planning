**Decisions made:**
- **The video was shot live-narrated, not silent-then-dubbed — a deliberate reversal of the shot list's plan.** The original design recorded picture and voice separately and laid the 14 source-quality announcement WAVs over the silent capture, timed to the 500 ms flash; that edit was named the plan's "long pole". The owner chose to narrate live and capture device audio digitally instead. Made workable by **scrcpy 4.1** mirroring the tablet to the PC with audio forwarding (Android 11+ required; the Lenovo runs Android 16), with OBS capturing the mirror window plus system audio and microphone on **separate tracks** — track 1 a usable mix, track 2 voice alone, track 3 device audio alone. The dubbing job disappeared entirely and the announcements arrived pre-synced. `adb screenrecord` was not used: it captures no audio at all, which was the whole reason the original plan existed.
- **The shot list was followed loosely and then abandoned for Act 2 onward.** The owner ad-libbed the dashboard walkthrough and skipped the full 14-minute Lockito journey, using the driver control panel to demonstrate announcement behaviour instead and saying so on camera. Recorded as an accepted deviation, not a defect: the result is a five-minute high-level product tour, which is the right register for the medium. Depth was moved to the written artefacts, which is why the README and case study carry more weight than originally scoped.
- **The infrastructure montage became screenshots, not video** (owner-decided, agent-agreed). Console tables are unreadable at a three-minute pace; as stills in a written case study a reader can stop and examine them. 16 captures taken across Supabase production, Sentry, Vercel and Firebase.
- **Four of the sixteen screenshots were published; the Firebase one was deliberately excluded** — it shows the real Firebase project IDs, which are on the workspace scrub list, and publishing it would have undone part of what the Phase 2b history rewrite achieved.
- **The case study is hand-written HTML in the product's own design language, not a Jekyll theme.** The project's claim to a documented, enforced design system is better demonstrated by rendering the page in Steelglass than asserted under someone else's stylesheet — and it leaves nothing to maintain on a repository about to be frozen.
- **Both documents lead with the compliance matrix rather than the stack.** Next.js and Kotlin are commodity claims; mapping a statutory instrument clause by clause onto requirements before writing code is not, and it is the claim that transfers to regulated work. The closure is stated exactly twice — a factual status line at the top and a short market analysis at the end — and never apologised for.
- **The video is a GitHub Release asset, not YouTube and not committed to the repository.** Free, permanent, no third-party account, and it keeps the artefact with the repository it documents. The cost is that it downloads rather than streams, which the page states plainly alongside the size and running time so nobody clicks a 92 MB file blind. Committing the MP4 into `docs/` would have streamed inline but permanently added 92 MB to a repository otherwise under a megabyte.
- **Supabase was paused, not deleted; everything else was deleted.** Pausing costs nothing, stops everything, and stays reversible — and the checksum-verified `pg_dump` means the outcome never depended on Supabase's retention policy either way.
- **GitHub Pages forced the publication order to flip.** Pages on a private repository requires a paid plan (confirmed by a 422 from the API, not assumed), so the repository had to go public *before* Pages could be enabled — the reverse of the conversion plan's sequence. Teardown still ran first so that the README's "infrastructure since decommissioned" was true at the moment of publication rather than aspirational.

Accepted knowingly: the released video is the single copy, four screenshots survive in this repository, the APK rebuilds from `pds-android`, and the announcement WAVs remain in paused Supabase Storage plus `pds-android/res/raw/`. **Verified:**
- **The pairing-screen defect was reproduced on hardware and root-caused, not guessed.** The screen rendered near-black text on the near-black canvas. The theme preference file did not exist on the tablet, so the store's `DEFAULT_IS_DARK = true` was in force and `DarkOnBackground` is `#EAEAEA` — the ink was therefore not coming from the scheme at all. Cause: `MainActivity`'s app-root `Scaffold` paints the Steelglass canvas with `containerColor = Color.Transparent` but named no `contentColor`, and M3 resolves that through `contentColorFor()`, which finds no scheme match for `Transparent` and falls back to `LocalContentColor` — whose Compose default outside any `Surface` is `Color.Black`. **Two screens were affected, not one:** `PairingScreen` and the FR-AT-60 `AccountStatusScreen`, whose entire lock message was invisible. `GmsWarningScreen` was unaffected because it already names its colours. Fixed at the root by naming `contentColor = colorScheme.onBackground` on the same Scaffold that paints the canvas.
- **Every figure quoted in the public documents was counted, not remembered.** 36 unique mapped clauses across eight regulations — not the 38 a naive row-grep returns, because two clauses appear in both a regulation table and the alert-tone table. 24 migrations, 9 Edge Functions, 8 of 8 tables RLS-enabled and 375,730 NaPTAN stops read from the live production project via the read-only MCP hours before it was paused.
- **Publication safety was swept twice.** Before writing: `git grep` over every tracked file for the full scrub list returned zero. After publication: a second sweep for JWTs, service-role key values, Sentry DSN keys, GitHub tokens, `AIza` API keys, private-key blocks and password assignments returned one hit — a documentation table naming the *variable* `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, no value. Only 45 markdown files, 4 vetted PNGs, the logo SVG, the HTML page and `.gitignore` are tracked.
- **Teardown was verified independently rather than trusted to the dashboards:** `pds-dashboard.com` and the Vercel deploy URL both return 404, the Vercel team lists zero projects and now reports plan `hobby` rather than `pro`, and the production Supabase REST endpoint does not respond.
- **The released video was verified as a real, retrievable file** after the local copies were deleted — HTTP 200 with `Content-Length` exactly 96,143,506 matching the original, and 2 MB pulled down whose header reads `ftypisom`, a valid MP4 container.
- **The case study was rendered and checked**, not just written: served locally, screenshotted at desktop and mobile widths, and then loaded from the live Pages URL with page and images both returning 200.
